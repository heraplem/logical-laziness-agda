\section{Correctness}\label{sec:equiv}

In this section, we prove the correctness of our translation by establishing
equivalence with the clairvoyance semantics and the demand
semantics~\cite{bjerner1989,demand-semantics}. We first define the clairvoyance
semantics on \(\LB\) by adapting the semantics of Hackett and Hutton on untyped
$\lambda$-calculus~\cite{clairvoyant} and that of Li \textit{et al.} on a typed
calculus~\cite{forking-paths} in \cref{subsec:clairvoyance}. We then define the
demand semantics on \(\LB\) by adapting the semantics of Bjerner and
Holmstr{\"{o}}m~\cite{bjerner1989} and Xia \textit{et
  al.}~\cite{demand-semantics} in \cref{subsec:demand-semantics}. We then prove
that our clairvoyance translation and demand translation produce terms in
\(\LL\) that compute the same result as the clairvoyance semantics and the
demand semantics on \(\LB\), respectively, in \cref{subsec:equiv}. These
theorems show the correctness of our translation. 

Our proofs rely on the equivalence relation between the clairvoyance semantics
and the demand semantics proven by Xia \textit{et al.}~\cite{demand-semantics},
which we take as axioms in our work. All our formalization, theorems, and proofs
are mechanized in Agda, which is provided as supplementary materials and will be
released as public-available artifact.\ly{TODO: Make sure we have the code
  materials ready for submission as well.}

\paragraph{Exact values and approximations.}
We first adapt concepts developed by Li \textit{et al.}~\cite{forking-paths} to
define the following: Each type \( A \) comes equipped with a set \( \Eval{A} \)
of \emph{exact values} and a set \( \Approx{A} \) of \emph{approximation
  values}, as defined in \cref{fig:exact-approx}. The set of exact values
\( \Eval{A} \) represents all possible values of $A$ where thunks have been
computed. The set of approximation values \( \Approx{A} \), on the other hand,
additionally include values where some thunks might be simply
``discarded''~(represented by $\sundefined$). These values represent ``smaller''
values where certain computations are not needed, \ie~they are approximations of
the exact values.\ly{TODO: Since \(\LB\) and \(\LL\) have different types now,

We additionally extend $\Eval{\cdot}$ and $\Approx{\cdot}$ to typing contexts.
We use $\Eval{\Gamma}$ to represent a typing context such that for any
$\gamma \in \Eval{\Gamma}$, $\gamma$ contains only exact values. The definition
of $\Approx{\Gamma}$ is similiar.

We can show that the evaluations semantics of \(\LB\) and that of \(\LL\)
satisfy the following properties:

\begin{lemma}[The evaluation semantics of \(\LB\) is an exact
  semantics]\label{lem:evaluation-exact}
  If \( \TB{\Gamma}{t}{A} \) and \( \gamma \in \Eval{\Gamma} \), then
  \( \EE{t}{\gamma} \in \Eval{A} \).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[The evaluation semantics of \(\LL\) is an approximation semantics]
  If \( \TL{\Gamma}{t}{A} \), \( \gamma \in \Approx{\Gamma} \), and
  \( \EL{\gamma}{t}{v} \), then \( v \in \Approx{A} \).
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Clairvoyance Semantics}\label{subsec:clairvoyance}

We define the clairvoyance semantics on \(\LB\) as a big-step operational
semantics that has the judgment schema \( \EC{\gamma}{t}{c}{v} \). Intuitively,
\( c \) is the number of \( \texttt{tick} \)s encountered while evaluating
\( t \) to \( v \). This way of defining the clairvoyance semantics is based on
the \emph{improvement theory}~\cite{moran1999}, just like the original
clairvoyance semantics defined by Hackett and Hutton~\cite{clairvoyant}, except
that we are based on a typed calculus \(\LB\) with explicit \( \tlazyZ \) and
\( \tforceZ \) operators.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \begin{mathpar}
  \inferrule{\EC{\gamma}{t}{c}{v}}{\EC{\gamma}{\tlazy{t}}{c}{\sthunk{v}}}{\textsc{C-Lazy-Result}} \qquad
  \inferrule{\\}{\EC{\gamma}{\tlazy{t}}{0}{\sundefined}}{\textsc{C-Lazy-Undefined}} \\
  \inferrule{\EC{\gamma}{t}{c}{\sthunk{v}}}{\EC{\gamma}{\tforce{t}}{c}{v}}{\textsc{C-Force}} \qquad
  \inferrule{\EC{\gamma}{t}{c}{v}}{\EC{\gamma}{\ttick{t}}{c + 1}{v}}{\textsc{C-Tick}} \\
   \inferrule{\EC{\gamma}{t_3}{c_1}{v_1} \\ \ECF{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{v_1}{c_2}{v_2}}{\EC{\gamma}{\tfoldr{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{t_3}}{c_1 + c_2}{v_2}}{\textsc{C-Foldr}} \\
\inferrule{\EC{\gamma}{t_2}{c}{v}}{\ECF{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{\snil}{c}{v}}{\textsc{C-Foldr-Nil}} \\
    \inferrule{\EC{\gamma, x_1 \mapsto v_1, x_2 \mapsto \sundefined}{t_1}{c}{v}}{\ECF{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{\scons{v_1}{\sundefined}}{c}{v}}{\textsc{C-Foldr-Undefined}} \\
    \inferrule{\ECF{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{v_2}{c_1}{v_3} \\ \EC{\gamma,
        x_1 \mapsto v_1, x_2 \mapsto
        \sthunk{v_3}}{t_1}{c_2}{v}}{\ECF{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{\scons{v_1}{\sthunk{v_2}}}{c_1
        + c_2}{v}}{\textsc{C-Foldr-Result}}
\end{mathpar}
\caption{A subset of the clairvoyance semantics defined on
  \(\LB\).}\label{fig:clairvoyance-base}
\end{figure}

We display the crucial rules of the clairvoyance semantics in
\cref{fig:clairvoyance-base}. The \rulename{C-Lazy-Result} and
\rulename{C-Lazy-Undefined} rules state that, to evaluate a \( \tlazyZ \) term,
we nondeterministically evaluate its subterm and wrap it in a
\( \sthunkZ \)~(\ie~\rulename{C-Lazy-Result}), or substitute it with an
``empty'' value \( \sundefined \)~(\ie~\rulename{C-Lazy-Undefined}). This is the
key for implementing clairvoyance semantics. The \rulename{C-Force} rule states
that we can only evaluate a value of \( \TThunkZ \) type if it is in fact a
\( \sthunkZ \), \ie~we cannot \( \tforceZ \) \( \sundefined \).

One major distinction between our semantics and that of Hackett and
Hutton~\cite{clairvoyant} is that, our base language \(\LB\) explicitly manages
laziness with \( \tlazyZ \) and \( \tforceZ \) operators, so we introduce
nondeterminism at the place of \( \tlazyZ \) operator. In contrast, Hackett and
Hutton~\cite{clairvoyant} introduce nondeterminism at \( \tletZ \) bindings:
when evaluating \( \tlet{x}{t_1}{t_2} \), either \( x \) was immediately bound
to the result of evaluting \( t_1 \), or else \( x \) was subtituted by
\( \sundefined \).

Note that \( \tfoldrZ \) is evaluated by first evaluting its list argument to a
value \(v\) and then iterating over \(v\) via the auxiliary relation
\( \ECF{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{v_2}{c_1}{v} \). Here, \( t_1 \) is a
``function'' (a term with free variables) that returns a \( \sthunkZ \) and
takes as arguments: (1)~an element of the list; and (2)~a \( \sthunkZ \)
obtained by iterating over the rest of the list, or \( \sundefined \) if the
rest of the list is itself \( \sundefined \).

\subsection{Demand Semantics}\label{subsec:demand-semantics}

The demand semantics is a deterministic semantics that interprets a term \( t \)
as a function \( \EDU{t} \) that takes two arguments: (1)~an \emph{exact}
evaluation context \( \gamma \), and (2)~a value of an approximation type that
represents the output demand \( d \) on the result of \( \EE{t}{\gamma} \); it
produces a list of minimal demands on \( \gamma \) and a computation cost. In
summary, demand semantics has the ``type'':
\[
  \EDU{-} : (t : \Gamma \vdash A) \to (\gamma : \Eval{\Gamma}) \times \Approx{A}_{\approxof{\relax}{\EE{t}{\gamma}}} \to \Approx{\Gamma}_{\approxof{\relax}{\gamma}} \times \N .
\]
Intuitively, demand sematics computes ``how much'' of the input \( \gamma \) is
required to compute a certain ``amount'' of the output \( \EE{t}{\gamma} \). It
also computes how much it costs to perform that computation along the way.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \begin{align*}
    \ED{x}{\gamma}{a} &= (\braces{x \mapsto a}, 0) \\
    \ED{\tlet{x}{t_1}{t_2}}{\gamma}{a} &= (d_1, c_1) \sqcup (d_2, c_2) &\text{where } ((d_2, x \mapsto b), c_2) &= \ED{t_2}{\gamma}{x \mapsto \EE{t}{\gamma}} \\
    &&\text{and } (d_1, c_1) &= \ED{t_1}{\gamma}{b} \\
    \ED{\ttick{t}}{\gamma}{a} &= (d, c + 1) &\text{where } (d, c) &= \ED{t}{\gamma}{a} \\
    \ED{\tlazy{t}}{\gamma}{\sundefined} &= (\sundefined, 0) \\
    \ED{\tlazy{t}}{\gamma}{\sthunk{a}} &= \ED{t}{\gamma}{a} \\
    \ED{\tforce{t}}{\gamma}{a} &= \ED{t}{\gamma}{\sthunk{a}} \\
    \ED{\ttrue}{\gamma}{a} &= (\sundefined, 0) \\
    \ED{\tfalse}{\gamma}{a} &= (\sundefined, 0) \\
    \ED{\tnil}{\gamma}{a} &= (\sundefined, 0) \\
    \ED{\tcons{t_1}{t_2}}{\gamma}{a} &= \ED{t_1}{\gamma}{a} \sqcup \ED{t_2}{\gamma}{a} \\
    \ED{\tfoldr{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{t_3}}{\gamma}{b} &= (d', c) \sqcup \ED{t}{\gamma}{b'} &\text{where } (g, b', c) &= \EDF{t_1}{t_2}{\gamma}{\EE{t_3}{\gamma}}{b}
  \end{align*}
  \caption{The demand semantics.\ly{Where is the definition of $\EDF{-}{-}{-}{-}{-}?$}}\label{fig:demand-semantics}
\end{figure}

We show the demand semantics on \(\LB\) in \cref{fig:demand-semantics}.

\begin{lemma}[Demand semantics is an approximation
  semantics]\label{prop:dem-approx-eval}
  Let \( (\gamma^A, c) = \ED{t}{\gamma}{a} \).  Then \( \approxof{\gamma^A}{\gamma} \).
\end{lemma}
Note that \cref{prop:dem-approx-eval} does not just state that the demand
semantics returns a value in $\Approx{\Gamma}_{\approxof{\relax}{\gamma}}$, it
also states that the value is an approximation of the input.

\subsection{Equivalence}\label{subsec:equiv}

\paragraph{The clairvoyance translation.}

\begin{theorem}\label{prop:equiv-clairvoyance}
  \( \EC{\gamma}{t}{c}{v} \) if and only if \( \EL{\gamma}{\floor{t}}{(v, c)} \).
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{The demand translation.}

The following two theorems demonstrate the equivalence of clairvoyance and
demand semantics. Let \( \Gamma \vdash t : A \), \( \gamma \in \Eval{\Gamma} \),
and \( \approxof{a^A}{\EE{t}{\gamma}} \); and define
\( (\gamma^A, c) = \ED{t}{\gamma}{a^A} \).

\begin{proposition}[Cost existence]\label{prop:cost-existence}
  Let \( \gamma^A \le {\gamma^A}' \prec \gamma \).  Then there exists some \( {a^A}' \ge a^A \) such that \( \EC{{\gamma^A}'}{t}{c}{{a^A}'} \).  In particular, \( \EC{\gamma^A}{t}{c}{a^A} \).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Cost minimality]\label{prop:cost-minimality}
  Let \( \approxof{{\gamma^A}'}{\gamma}, {a^A}' \ge a^A, c' \in \N \) such that \( \EC{{\gamma^A}'}{t}{c'}{{a^A}'} \). Then \( ({\gamma^A}', c') \ge (\gamma^A, c) \).
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}
  Let \( \Gamma = x_1 : A_1, \ldots, x_n : A_n \).  If \( \TB{\Gamma}{t}{B} \)
  and \( \gamma \in \Eval{\Gamma} \), then
  \( \TL{\Gamma, y^A : B}{\transD{t}{\Gamma}}{\TM{(\texttt{\(A_1\)\ *\
        \(\cdots\) *\ \(A_n\)})}} \),
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Semantics of the demand translation]\label{prop:dem-trans-sem}
  \( \EL{\gamma, y^A \mapsto a^A}{\transD{t}{\Gamma}}{(\verts{\gamma^A}, c)} \)
  if and only if (1)~\( \gamma^A \le \gamma \), and (2)~\(  \EC{\gamma^A}{t}{c}{a^A} \).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  By construction, applying \cref{prop:equiv-clairvoyance}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Adequacy of the demand translation]
  Define \( (\gamma^A, c) = \ED{t}{\gamma}{a^A} \) and let \( {\gamma^A}' \ge \gamma^A \).  Then there exists some \( {a^A}' \ge a^A \) such that \( \EL{\gamma, y^A \mapsto {a^A}'}{\transD{t}{\Gamma}}{({\gamma^A}', c)} \).  In particular, \( \EL{\gamma, y^A \mapsto a^A}{\transD{t}{\Gamma}}{(\gamma^A, c)} \).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  By \cref{prop:dem-trans-sem} and \cref{prop:cost-existence}.
\end{proof}

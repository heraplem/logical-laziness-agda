\paragraph{Lazy cost analysis.}
Existing research on lazy cost analysis can be divided into roughly three
different approaches. The first approach, represented by work on
Iris$^{\$}$~\cite{iris-credit,iris-thunk}, deals with mutable heaps directly,
but requires using special programming logics like the separation
logic~\cite{separation-logic}. The second approach, represented by
LiquidHaskell~\cite{liquidate,liquid-okasaki}, uses a graded monad proposed by
Danielsson~\cite{danielsson-08} to track computation cost. However, this
approach needs to deal with sharing manually. The third approach utilizes
alternative semantics, such as clairvoyance
semantics~\cite{clairvoyant,forking-paths} and demand
semantics~\cite{bjerner1989,demand-semantics}, that are equivalent to lazy
evaluation in terms of computation cost. In contrast to the natural semantics of
lazy evaluation~\cite{launchbury1993}, these semantics reify computation cost in
pure and eager semantics, so we can use them to reason about lazy computation
cost without being concerned with mutable heaps, sharing, or our-of-order
executions.

Clairvoyance semantics was proposed by Hackett and Hutton~\cite{clairvoyant},
who proved its equivalence with Launchbury's semantics. Li \emph{et al.}
extended clairvoyance semantics to a typed setting and mechanized it in Rocq,
along with a framework for mechanically reasoning computation
costs~\cite{forking-paths}. Demand semantics was proposed by Bjerner and
Holmstr{\"{o}}m~\cite{bjerner1989}. Xia \emph{et al.} later extended it to a
typed setting and proved its equivalence to clairvoyance semantics, in forms of
cost existence and cost minimality theorems~\cite{demand-semantics}. We rely on
these results in our correctness theorems in \cref{sec:equiv}. They further use
demand semantics to mechanically verify amortized costs and persistence of two
of Okasaki's data structures: the banker's queue and the implicit
queue~\cite{purely-functional} in Rocq. Recently, a third semantics, memorist
semantics, was proposed by Li \emph{et al.}~\cite{memorist}. Memorist semantics
works by tracking all the computation dependencies. As it works differently than
clairvoyance semantics and demand semantics, it is unclear if there's a
connection we can draw like in this paper.

There has also been work on applying property-based testing to lazy
computation cost analysis, such as Foner \textit{et al.}~\cite{foner2018} and
Lorenzen~\cite{lazy-amortized-testing}. We leave it as future work to extend our
work to property-based testing frameworks in Curry like
EasyCheck~\cite{easy-check} and CurryCheck~\cite{curry-check}.

\paragraph{Functional logic programming.}
We implemented our examples in Curry, but most of the techniques in this paper
are not specific to Curry. Other functional logic languages, such as
Verse~\cite{verse} or Mercury~\cite{mercury}, could also have proven sufficient.
Because \(\LL\) uses eager evaluation, we could have even translated to a pure
logic language such as Prolog~\cite{EagerFLPIsLogic}. However, the search tree
traversal employed in this work is
Curry-specific~\cite{DBLP:journals/jflp/BrasselHH04}.

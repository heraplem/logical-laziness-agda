\paragraph{Exact values and approximations.}

Each type \( A \) comes equipped with a set \( \Eval{A} \) of \emph{exact
  values} and a set \( \Approx{A} \) of \emph{approximation values}, as defined
in \cref{fig:exact-approx}.\footnote{These concepts are borrowed from same
  concepts in Li et al.~\cite{forking-paths}.} The set of exact values
\( \Eval{A} \) represents all possible values of $A$ where thunks have been
\( \tforceZ \)d. The set of approximation values \( \Approx{A} \), on the other
hand, additionally include values where some thunks might be simply
``discarded''~(represented by $\sundefined$). These values represent ``smaller''
values where certain computations are not needed, \ie~they are approximations of
the exact values.

We additionally extend $\Eval{\cdot}$ and $\Approx{\cdot}$ to typing contexts.
We use $\Eval{\Gamma}$ to represent a typing context such that for any
$\gamma \in \Eval{\Gamma}$, $\gamma$ contains only exact values. The definition
of $\Approx{\Gamma}$ is similiar.

\begin{figure}[t]
\begin{center}
  \begin{align*}
    %\Eval{A} &\in \mathcal{P}(A) \\
    \Eval{\TBool} &= \SBool \\
    \Eval{\TThunk{A}} &= \Eval{A} \\
    \Eval{\TList{A}} &= \braces{\snil} \cup \setbuilder{\scons{v_1}{v_2}}{v_1 \in \Eval{A}, v_2 \in \Eval{\TList{A}}} \\ \\
    %\Approx{A} &\in \mathcal{P}(A) \\
    \Approx{\TBool} &= \SBool \\
    \Approx{\TThunk{A}} &= \set{\sundefined} \cup \setbuilder{\sthunk{v}}{v \in \Approx{A}} \\
    \Approx{\TList{A}} &= \set{\snil} \cup \setbuilder{\scons{v_1}{v_2}}{v_1 \in \Approx{A}, v_2 \in \Approx{\TThunk{(\TList{A})}}}
  \end{align*}
\end{center}

  represent the power set of $A$.}\label{fig:exact-approx}
\end{figure}


\begin{lemma}[Evaluation semantics is an exact
  semantics]\label{lem:evaluation-exact}
  If \( \TB{\Gamma}{t}{A} \) and \( \gamma \in \Eval{\Gamma} \), then \( \EE{t}{\gamma} \in \Eval{A} \).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}
  If \( \TL{\Gamma}{t}{A} \), \( \gamma \in \Approx{\Gamma} \), and \( \EL{\gamma}{t}{v} \), then \( v \in \Approx{A} \).
\end{lemma}


\subsection{Clairvoyance semantics}

We define the clairvoyance semantics on $B$ as a big-step operational semantics
that has the judgment schema \( \EC{\gamma}{t}{c}{v} \). Intuitively, \( c \) is
the number of \( \texttt{tick} \)s encountered while evaluating \( t \) to
\( v \). This way of defining the clairvoyance semantics is based on the
\emph{improvement theory}~\cite{moran1999}, just like the original clairvoyance
semantics defined by Hackett and Hutton~\cite{clairvoyant}, except that we are
based on a typed calculus $B$ with explicit \( \tlazyZ \) and \( \tforceZ \)
operators.

  \begin{mathpar}
  \inferrule{\EC{\gamma}{t}{c}{v}}{\EC{\gamma}{\tlazy{t}}{c}{\sthunk{v}}}{\textsc{C-Lazy-Result}} \qquad
  \inferrule{\\}{\EC{\gamma}{\tlazy{t}}{0}{\sundefined}}{\textsc{C-Lazy-Undefined}} \\
  \inferrule{\EC{\gamma}{t}{c}{\sthunk{v}}}{\EC{\gamma}{\tforce{t}}{c}{v}}{\textsc{C-Force}} \qquad
  \inferrule{\EC{\gamma}{t}{c}{v}}{\EC{\gamma}{\ttick{t}}{c + 1}{v}}{\textsc{C-Tick}} \\
   \inferrule{\EC{\gamma}{t_3}{c_1}{v_1} \\ \ECF{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{v_1}{c_2}{v_2}}{\EC{\gamma}{\tfoldr{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{t_3}}{c_1 + c_2}{v_2}}{\textsc{C-Foldr}} \\
\inferrule{\EC{\gamma}{t_2}{c}{v}}{\ECF{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{\snil}{c}{v}}{\textsc{C-Foldr-Nil}} \\
    \inferrule{\EC{\gamma, x_1 \mapsto v_1, x_2 \mapsto \sundefined}{t_1}{c}{v}}{\ECF{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{\scons{v_1}{\sundefined}}{c}{v}}{\textsc{C-Foldr-Undefined}} \\
    \inferrule{\ECF{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{v_2}{c_1}{v_3} \\ \EC{\gamma,
        x_1 \mapsto v_1, x_2 \mapsto
        \sthunk{v_3}}{t_1}{c_2}{v}}{\ECF{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{\scons{v_1}{\sthunk{v_2}}}{c_1
        + c_2}{v}}{\textsc{C-Foldr-Result}}
\end{mathpar}
\caption{A subset of the clairvoyance semantics defined on
  $B$.}\label{fig:clairvoyance-base}
\end{figure}

We display the crucial rules of the clairvoyance semantics in
\cref{fig:clairvoyance-base}. The \rulename{C-Lazy-Result} and
\rulename{C-Lazy-Undefined} rules state that, to evaluate a \( \tlazyZ \) term,
we nondeterministically evaluate its subterm and wrap it in a
\( \sthunkZ \)~(\ie~\rulename{C-Lazy-Result}), or substitute it with an
``empty'' value \( \sundefined \)~(\ie~\rulename{C-Lazy-Undefined}). This is the
key for implementing clairvoyance semantics. The \rulename{C-Force} rule states
that we can only evaluate a value of \( \TThunkZ \) type if it is in fact a
\( \sthunkZ \),\ie~we cannot \( \tforceZ \) \( \sundefined \).

One major distinction between our semantics and that of Hackett and
Hutton~\cite{clairvoyant} is that, our base language $B$ explicitly manages
laziness with \( \tlazyZ \) and \( \tforceZ \) operators, so we introduce
nondeterminism at the place of \( \tlazyZ \) operator. In contrast, Hackett and
Hutton~\cite{clairvoyant} introduce nondeterminism at \( \tletZ \) bindings:
when evaluating \( \tlet{x}{t_1}{t_2} \), either \( x \) was immediately bound
to the result of evaluting \( t_1 \), or else \( x \) was subtituted by
\( \sundefined \).

\subsection{Demand semantics}

The demand semantics is a deterministic semantics that interprets a term \( t \)
as a function \( \EDU{t} \) that takes two arguments:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a fully-defined evaluation context \( \gamma \), and
\item a demand \( d \) on \( \EE{t}{\gamma} \);
\end{enumerate}
it produces a list of demands on \( \gamma \) and an evaluation cost. In summary, demand semantics has the ``type''
\[
  \EDU{-} : (t : \Gamma \vdash A) \to (\gamma : \Eval{\Gamma}) \times \Approx{A}_{\approxof{\relax}{\EE{t}{\gamma}}} \to \Approx{\Gamma}_{\approxof{\relax}{\gamma}} \times \N .
\]
Intuitively, demand sematics computes ``how much'' of the input \( \gamma \) must be present in order to compute a certain ``amount'' of the output \( \EE{t}{\gamma} \), and also how much it costs to perform that computation.

\begin{figure}\centering
  \begin{align*}
    \ED{x}{\gamma}{a} &= (\braces{x \mapsto a}, 0) \\
    \ED{\tlet{x}{t_1}{t_2}}{\gamma}{a} &= (d_1, c_1) \sqcup (d_2, c_2) &\text{where } ((d_2, x \mapsto b), c_2) &= \ED{t_2}{\gamma}{x \mapsto \EE{t}{\gamma}} \\
    &&\text{and } (d_1, c_1) &= \ED{t_1}{\gamma}{b} \\
    \ED{\ttick{t}}{\gamma}{a} &= (d, c + 1) &\text{where } (d, c) &= \ED{t}{\gamma}{a} \\
    \ED{\tlazy{t}}{\gamma}{\sundefined} &= (\sundefined, 0) \\
    \ED{\tlazy{t}}{\gamma}{\sthunk{a}} &= \ED{t}{\gamma}{a} \\
    \ED{\tforce{t}}{\gamma}{a} &= \ED{t}{\gamma}{\sthunk{a}} \\
    \ED{\ttrue}{\gamma}{a} &= (\sundefined, 0) \\
    \ED{\tfalse}{\gamma}{a} &= (\sundefined, 0) \\
    \ED{\tnil}{\gamma}{a} &= (\sundefined, 0) \\
    \ED{\tcons{t_1}{t_2}}{\gamma}{a} &= \ED{t_1}{\gamma}{a} \sqcup \ED{t_2}{\gamma}{a} \\
    \ED{\tfoldr{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{t_3}}{\gamma}{b} &= (d', c) \sqcup \ED{t}{\gamma}{b'} &\text{where } (g, b', c) &= \EDF{t_1}{t_2}{\gamma}{\EE{t_3}{\gamma}}{b}
  \end{align*}

\subsection{Equivalence}

The following two theorems demonstrate the equivalence of clairvoyance and demand semantics. Let \( \Gamma \vdash t : A \), \( \gamma \in \Eval{\Gamma} \), and \( \approxof{a^A}{\EE{t}{\gamma}} \); and define \( (\gamma^A, c) = \ED{t}{\gamma}{a^A} \).

\begin{theorem}[Cost existence]
  \label{prop:cost-existence}
  Let \( \gamma^A \le {\gamma^A}' \prec \gamma \).  Then there exists some \( {a^A}' \ge a^A \) such that \( \EC{{\gamma^A}'}{t}{c}{{a^A}'} \).  In particular, \( \EC{\gamma^A}{t}{c}{a^A} \).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Cost minimality]
  \label{prop:cost-minimality}
  Let \( \approxof{{\gamma^A}'}{\gamma}, {a^A}' \ge a^A, c' \in \N \) such that \( \EC{{\gamma^A}'}{t}{c'}{{a^A}'} \). Then \( ({\gamma^A}', c') \ge (\gamma^A, c) \).
\end{theorem}


\paragraph{Clairvoyance translation}

\begin{theorem}\label{prop:equiv-clairvoyance}
  \( \EC{\gamma}{t}{c}{v} \) if and only if \( \EL{\gamma}{\floor{t}}{(v, c)} \).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  (\(\implies\)) By induction on the derivation of \( \EC{\gamma}{t}{c}{v} \).

  \textbf{Case:}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{\EC{\gamma}{t_3}{c_1}{v_1}}
      \hypo{\ECF{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{v_1}{c_2}{v_2}}
      \infer2[\rulename{L-Foldr}]{\EC{\gamma}{\tfoldr{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{t_3}}{c_1 + c_2}{v_2}}
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{center}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \( t = \tfoldr{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{t_3} \)
  \item \( \floor{t} = \texttt{do \(y_3\) <- \(\floor{t_3}\); \tfoldM{x_1}{x_2}{\floor{t_1}}{\floor{t_2}}{y_3}} \)
  \end{itemize}
  We need to construct a derivation of the form
  \begin{center}
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{\EL{\gamma}{\floor{t_3}}{\spair{v_1}{c_1}}}
      \hypo{\EL{\gamma, y_3 \mapsto v_1}{\tfoldM{x_1}{x_2}{\floor{t_1}}{\floor{t_2}}{y_3}}{\spair{v_2}{c_2}}}
      \infer2[\rulename{L-Bind}]{\EL{\gamma}{\texttt{do \(y_3\) <- \(\floor{t_3}\); \tfoldM{x_1}{x_2}{\floor{t_1}}{\floor{t_2}}{y_3}}{\spair{v_2}{c_2}}}{\spair{v_2}{c_1 + c_2}}}
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{center}
  By the inductive hypothesis, we have
  \[
    \EL{\gamma}{\floor{t_3}}{(v_1, c_1)} ,
  \]
  so we just need to prove
  \[
    \EL{\gamma, y_3 \mapsto v_1}{\tfoldM{x_1}{x_2}{\floor{t_1}}{\floor{t_2}}{y_3}}{\floor{t_2}}{y_3}{\spair{v_2}{c_2}} .
  \]
  For this, it suffices to prove
  \[
    \ELF{\gamma}{x_1}{y_2}{\tfoldM{x_1}{x_2}{\floor{t_1}}{\floor{t_2}}{y_3}}{\floor{t_2}}{y_3}{\spair{v_2}{c_2}} .
  \]
  We must also prove the following lemma by mutual induction.
  \begin{lemma}
    \( \ECF{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{v_1}{c}{v_2} \) if and only if \( \ELFM{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{\floor{t_1}}{\floor{t_2}}{v_1}{\spair{v_2}{c}} \).
  \end{lemma}
  \begin{proof}
    By induction on the derivation of \( \ECF{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{t_1}{t_2}{v_1}{c}{v_2} \).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Case \rulename{C-Foldr-Nil}:}
    \begin{itemize}
    \item \( v_1 = \varepsilon \)
    \item \( \EC{\gamma}{t_2}{c}{v_2} \)
    \end{itemize}
    By mutual induction, we obtain \( \EL{\gamma}{\floor{t_2}}{\spair{v_2}{c}} \), so we can construct
    \begin{center}
      \begin{prooftree}
        \hypo{\EL{\gamma}{\floor{t_2}}{\spair{v_2}{c}}}
        \infer1[\rulename{L-FoldM-Nil}]{\ELF{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{\floor{t_1}}{\floor{t_2}}{\snil}{\spair{v_2}{c}}}
      \end{prooftree}
    \end{center}
  \item \textbf{Case \rulename{L-Foldr-Undefined}}:
  \end{itemize}

  (\(\impliedby\)) By induction on the derivation of \( \ELFM{\gamma}{x_1}{x_2}{\floor{t_1}}{\floor{t_2}}{v_1}{\spair{v_2}{c}} \).
  % \end{proof}

\end{proof}

\paragraph{Demand translation}


\begin{lemma}
  Let \( \Gamma = x_1 : A_1, \ldots, x_n : A_n \).  If \( \TB{\Gamma}{t}{B} \) and \( \gamma \in \Eval{\Gamma} \), then \( \TL{\Gamma, y^A : B}{\transD{t}{\Gamma}}{\TM{(\texttt{\(A_1\)\ *\ \(\cdots\) *\ \(A_n\)})}} \)
\end{lemma}

Define \( \verts{x_1 \mapsto a_1, \ldots, x_n \mapsto a_n} = (a_1, \ldots, a_n) \).

\begin{lemma}[Semantics of the demand translation]\label{prop:dem-trans-sem}
  \( \EL{\gamma, y^A \mapsto a^A}{\transD{t}{\Gamma}}{(\verts{\gamma^A}, c)} \) if and only if
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item \( \gamma^A \le \gamma \), and
  \item \(  \EC{\gamma^A}{t}{c}{a^A} \).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  By construction, applying \cref{prop:equiv-clairvoyance}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Adequacy of the demand translation]
  Define \( (\gamma^A, c) = \ED{t}{\gamma}{a^A} \) and let \( {\gamma^A}' \ge \gamma^A \).  Then there exists some \( {a^A}' \ge a^A \) such that \( \EL{\gamma, y^A \mapsto {a^A}'}{\transD{t}{\Gamma}}{({\gamma^A}', c)} \).  In particular, \( \EL{\gamma, y^A \mapsto a^A}{\transD{t}{\Gamma}}{(\gamma^A, c)} \).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  By \cref{prop:dem-trans-sem} and \cref{prop:cost-existence}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Soundness of the demand translation]
  If \( \EL{\gamma, y^A \mapsto a^A}{\transD{t}{\Gamma}}{(\verts{\gamma^A}, c)} \), then \( \ED{t}{\gamma}{a^A} \le (\gamma^A, c) \).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  By \cref{prop:dem-trans-sem} and \cref{prop:cost-minimality}.
\end{proof}

It is slightly unsatisfying that the demand translation does not in general reduce \emph{only} to the actual result of demand semantics. Some logic languages have a built-in facility to collapse a nondeterministic computation into a deterministic one, returning either the first result found or a list of all possible results. If we can arrange for less-defined results to always be tried first, we can use such a facility to ensure that the demand translation does in fact always return the actual result of demand semantics.

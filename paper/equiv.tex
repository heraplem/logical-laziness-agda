We show an overview of how we formally prove correctness theorems in
\cref{fig:proof-overview}. We first define clairvoyance semantics
\(\SC\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket\) on \(\LB\). We then show that starting from a
term \(t\) in \(\LB\), we can translate it to a term in \(\LL\) using the
\transCname{} \(\transC{t}\) that has the same meaning as
\(\SC\llbracket t \rrbracket\). We also prove a similar theorem relating demand
semantics to the \transDname.

\subsection{The \transCname{}}

Clairvoyance semantics is a big-step operational semantics with the judgment
schema \(\EC{\gamma}{t}{c}{v}\). Intuitively, \(c\) is the number of
\(\ttickZ\)s encountered while evaluating \(t\) to \(v\). Our semantics is
similar to the original clairvoyance semantics of Hackett and
Hutton~\cite{clairvoyant}; e.g., like their semantics, ours is based on
improvement theory~\cite{moran1999}. The most significant difference is that,
unlike Hackett and Hutton's calculus, our calculus has explicit \(\tlazyZ\) and
\(\tforceZ\) operators. Therefore, whereas Hackett and Hutton introduce
nondeterminism at \(\tletZ\) bindings, we must introduce it at \(\tlazyZ\)
terms. Readers can find detailed definitions of our clairvoyance semantics in
\cref{apx:clairvoyance-semantics} and in our artifact.
% when evaluating \( \tlet{x}{t_1}{t_2} \) in their semantics, either \( x \) is
% either immediately bound to the result of evaluating \( t_1 \), or it s not
% bound at all.

\begin{theorem}[Correctness of the \transCname]\label{prop:equiv-clairvoyance}
  \( \EC{\gamma}{t}{c}{v} \) if and only if \( \EL{\gamma}{\floor{t}}{(v, c)} \).
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The \transDname{}}\label{subsec:values}

Demand semantics is a deterministic semantics that interprets a term \( t \) as
a function \( \EDU{t} \) that takes two arguments: an \emph{exact} evaluation
context \( \gamma \) (containing only values with no \(\sundefined\) parts) and
an \emph{approximation} value that represents the demand on the result of
\( \EE{t}{\gamma} \). It produces a list \(\gamma^A\) of minimal demands on
\(\gamma\) and a computation cost. Intuitively, demand semantics computes ``how
much'' of the input \( \gamma \) is required to compute a desired ``amount'' of
the output \( \EE{t}{\gamma} \) and how much it costs to perform the
computation.

We omit detailed definitions of demand semantics on \(\LB\), as it is
essentially the same as that of Xia \textit{et al.}~\cite{demand-semantics}.
Interested readers can refer to them in \cref{apx:demand-semantics} and in our
artifact.

\begin{lemma}[Semantics of the \transDname]\label{prop:dem-trans-sem}
  \( \EL{\gamma, y^A \mapsto a^A}{\transD{t}{\Gamma}}{(\verts{\gamma^A}, c)} \)
  if and only if (1)~\( \gamma^A \le \gamma \), and
  (2)~\( \EC{\gamma^A}{t}{c}{a^A} \).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  By construction, applying \cref{prop:equiv-clairvoyance}.
\end{proof}

We can now establish the correctness of the \transDname{} via two theorems:
adequacy and soundness.
\begin{theorem}[Adequacy of the \transDname]
  Define \( (\gamma^A, c) = \ED{t}{\gamma}{a^A} \) and let
  \( {\gamma^A}' \ge \gamma^A \). Then there exists some \( {a^A}' \ge a^A \)
  such that
  \( \EL{\gamma, y^A \mapsto {a^A}'}{\transD{t}{\Gamma}}{({\gamma^A}', c)} \).
\end{theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Soundness of the \transDname]
  If
  \( \EL{\gamma, y^A \mapsto a^A}{\transD{t}{\Gamma}}{(\verts{\gamma^A}, c)} \),
  then \( \ED{t}{\gamma}{a^A} \le (\gamma^A, c) \).
\end{theorem}
The proofs of these theorems depend on \cref{prop:dem-trans-sem} and the
theorems of cost existence and cost minimality in Xia \textit{et
  al.}~\cite{demand-semantics}.

In functional logic programming, one program contains two semantics: a "forward"
nondeterministic semantics with choices, and a "backward" demand-driven
semantics with free variables. But why is that useful? In this paper, we show
that two independently discovered semantics that model the computation cost of
lazy functional programs, namely the clairvoyance semantics of Hackett and
Hutton and the demand semantics of Bjerner and Holmström, correspond exactly to
these two semantics of one functional logic program. We propose and formalize a
mechanical translation strategy based on a simple typed calculus that produces a
clairvoyance translation based on a simple logic language, and a demand
translation that is a simple wrapper of the clairvoyance translation. We prove
the translation strategy to be correct in Agda. We also conduct case studies
based on common lazy functional programs to demonstrate how this approach can be
applied to Curry/PEKCS.

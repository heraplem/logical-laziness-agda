We show an overview of how we formally prove correctness theorems in
\cref{fig:proof-overview}. We first define clairvoyance semantics
\(\SC\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket\) on \(\LB\). We then show that starting from a
term \(t\) in \(\LB\), we can translate it to a term in \(\LL\) using the
\transCname{} \(\transC{t}\), that has the same meaning as
\(\SC\llbracket t \rrbracket\). We also show a similar theorem between demand
semantics and the \transDname.

We define clairvoyance semantics on \(\LB\) as a big-step operational semantics
with the judgment schema \(\EC{\gamma}{t}{c}{v}\): intuitively, \(c\) is the
number of \(\ttickZ\)s encountered while evaluating \(t\) to \(v\). As with
Hackett and Hutton's original clairvoyance semantics, our definition is based on
\emph{improvement theory}~\cite{moran1999}. We omit detailed definitions of
clairvoyance semantics on \(\LB\) here because our semantics is essentially the
same as that of Hackett and Hutton~\cite{clairvoyant}, except that we are based
on a typed calculus with explicit \( \tlazyZ \) and \( \tforceZ \) operators.

\begin{theorem}[Correctness of the \transCname]\label{prop:equiv-clairvoyance}
  \( \EC{\gamma}{t}{c}{v} \) if and only if \( \EL{\gamma}{\floor{t}}{(v, c)} \).
\end{theorem}

Demand semantics is a deterministic semantics that interprets a term \( t \)
as a function \( \EDU{t} \) which takes two arguments: an \emph{exact}
evaluation context \( \gamma \), and an approximation value that represents
the output demand on the result of \( \EE{t}{\gamma} \). It produces a list of
minimal demands on \( \gamma \) and a computation cost. In summary, demand
semantics has the ``type'':
\[
  \EDU{-} : (t : \Gamma \vdash A) \to (\gamma : \Eval{\Gamma}) \times \Approx{A}_{\approxof{\relax}{\EE{t}{\gamma}}} \to \Approx{\Gamma}_{\approxof{\relax}{\gamma}} \times \N .
\]
Intuitively, demand semantics computes ``how much'' of the input \( \gamma \) is
required to compute a desired ``amount'' of the output \( \EE{t}{\gamma} \). It
also computes how much it costs to perform the computation.

We omit detailed definitions of demand semantics on \(\LB\) as it is essentially
the same as that of Xia \textit{et al.}~\cite{demand-semantics}, but interested
readers can refer to them in \cref{apx:demand-semantics} and in our artifact.

Our proof of the correctness of the \transDname{} builds on the following
results proven by Xia \textit{et al.}~\cite{demand-semantics}, which we take as
axioms in our Agda formalization (the proof of Xia \textit{et al.} was in
Rocq~\cite{coq}): Let \( \Gamma \vdash t : A \), \( \gamma \in \Eval{\Gamma} \),
and \( \approxof{a^A}{\EE{t}{\gamma}} \); and define
\( (\gamma^A, c) = \ED{t}{\gamma}{a^A} \).

\begin{proposition}[Cost existence]\label{prop:cost-existence}
  Let \( \gamma^A \le {\gamma^A}' \prec \gamma \).  Then there exists some \( {a^A}' \ge a^A \) such that \( \EC{{\gamma^A}'}{t}{c}{{a^A}'} \).  In particular, \( \EC{\gamma^A}{t}{c}{a^A} \).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Cost minimality]\label{prop:cost-minimality}
  Let \( \approxof{{\gamma^A}'}{\gamma}, {a^A}' \ge a^A, c' \in \N \) such that \( \EC{{\gamma^A}'}{t}{c'}{{a^A}'} \). Then \( ({\gamma^A}', c') \ge (\gamma^A, c) \).
\end{proposition}

\Cref{prop:cost-existence,prop:cost-minimality} establish equivalence between
clairvoyance semantics and demand semantics. Together with
\cref{prop:equiv-clairvoyance}, we can show that the \transDname{}, which relies
on the \transCname, is equivalent to the demand semantics.

\begin{lemma}[Semantics of the \transDname]\label{prop:dem-trans-sem}
  \( \EL{\gamma, y^A \mapsto a^A}{\transD{t}{\Gamma}}{(\verts{\gamma^A}, c)} \)
  if and only if (1)~\( \gamma^A \le \gamma \), and (2)~\(  \EC{\gamma^A}{t}{c}{a^A} \).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  By construction, applying \cref{prop:equiv-clairvoyance}.
\end{proof}

We can then establish the correctness of the \transDname{} via two theorems:
adequacy and soundness:

\begin{theorem}[Adequacy of the \transDname]
  Define \( (\gamma^A, c) = \ED{t}{\gamma}{a^A} \) and let \( {\gamma^A}' \ge \gamma^A \).  Then there exists some \( {a^A}' \ge a^A \) such that \( \EL{\gamma, y^A \mapsto {a^A}'}{\transD{t}{\Gamma}}{({\gamma^A}', c)} \).  In particular, \( \EL{\gamma, y^A \mapsto a^A}{\transD{t}{\Gamma}}{(\gamma^A, c)} \).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  By \cref{prop:dem-trans-sem,prop:cost-existence}.
\end{proof}
